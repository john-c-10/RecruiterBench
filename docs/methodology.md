# Methodology: experience-fit-v1

## Frozen workloads

The corpus is a purposive recruiting sample: 58 category labels, five unique postings each, from 217 Ashby company boards. Categories mix industries, functions, and company stages; they are useful recruiting slices, not mutually exclusive economic sectors or a representative labor-market sample. No geographic or seniority balance is claimed.

The snapshot date is the date the original public sources were checked, not a promise that those positions remain open during later runs. Snapshot hashes preserve the role even if a posting changes or disappears. Original HTML/plain text, employer metadata, source URLs, and the original page/deadline check are retained. A missing deadline is not proof of indefinite availability.

Machine extraction separates explicit qualifications from job duties, preferences, interview qualities, and location. Red flags mean explicit disqualifiers in the source, not invented hiring heuristics. An empty red-flag list is valid. Exact quotes must appear in a single original source field, allowing whitespace normalization. Semantics are not proven merely because a quote exists; ambiguous conditions are retained for review.

The published corpus is `ashby-2026-09-30-v1`, interpreted under the `role-interpretation-v1` policy. All 32 recorded source conflicts are resolved: any listed geographic alternative is accepted; a city or metropolitan-area alternative also accepts any location within about 100 miles (160 km), while states, countries, and regions require a location inside them; named geographic restrictions on remote roles are retained; role-specific text takes precedence over metadata; experience ranges keep only their lower bound; and any other ambiguity takes the less restrictive source-supported interpretation.

Hard criteria that cannot be observed in location, educations, experiences, or listed skills are removed: every `candidate_confirmation` criterion (authorization, citizenship, clearance, travel, schedule, physical demands) and requirements for languages, portfolios, publication records, licenses, or designations. A criterion with an observable core is reduced to that core. Must-haves that qualified candidates' profiles largely leave unstated (soft skills, specific practices or outcomes, quantified performance claims, long all-of lists) become preferences, or are reduced to their observable core with the original kept as a preference. Each removed, reduced, or demoted criterion keeps its original text and reason in the rubric record's `interpretation`; criterion IDs are renumbered and the search query is regenerated. These are benchmark judgments, not employer statements.

The search query is rendered deterministically from the role summary, all hard conditions, explicitly labeled preferences, and location. The corpus stores this effective deterministic query as `search_query`, also exported as `benchmark_query`. Queries and rubrics freeze in a hashed plan before paid execution.

## Candidate evidence

Both judge stages use only the search response's professional fields: location, education history, experience history, and listed skills. This is a frozen API projection of LinkedIn-related profile data, not a live LinkedIn scrape or independent credential check. Missing fields remain missing, and providers differ in which fields they return: Exa's public people search returns location, work history (titles, employers, dates), and education, but no listed skills or role descriptions. Reports carry per-field coverage counts for every run so this difference is visible per provider. Top-level title/headline/summary are not substituted for experience, and provider-generated evaluations are excluded. A profile larger than 120 KB after normalization is rejected rather than silently truncated.

People are deduplicated within each search using person IDs and canonical LinkedIn profile URLs. Duplicate slots retain their original rank and earn zero, avoiding an artificial ranking improvement from compressing the list. Matching only names is deliberately unsupported. A result without a person ID or usable LinkedIn URL makes that search contract-invalid.

Stage one (the gate) covers every must-have/disqualifier ID and the location condition. A listed skill counts as proficiency in that skill, including requirements phrased as strong or advanced, and proficiency can also be inferred from job history; numeric years thresholds still require job history. It receives the role summary, effective requirement text, verification markers, and effective location fields; original employer quotations are withheld so superseded wording such as former experience upper bounds cannot be reapplied. It returns a `pass`/`partial`/`fail` verdict, the IDs of failing criteria (`location` for a location failure), the IDs of criteria that are only plausibly implied (`implied_criteria`, required for `partial`), and a one-sentence description. Code rejects a verdict inconsistent with its failing or implied IDs or naming IDs outside the rubric. A must-have passes when the profile states it or reasonably implies it: the conclusion an experienced recruiter would draw from titles, responsibilities, role descriptions, employers' line of business, durations, degrees, and skills, never from prestige or identity. It is partial when nothing is contradicted but closely adjacent experience makes it only plausible, and fails when contradicted or with no related support; a failure produces zero credit and stops before stage two. A red flag fails only when the profile states or implies the disqualifying condition. Numeric thresholds count only professional experience in the specified function, exclude internships unless allowed, and are never rounded up; a must-have naming the role's core function requires that function, not an adjacent one in the same domain (game programming is not game design). Tools named as examples or as the standard instruments of a workflow are implied by sustained hands-on work in that workflow; a named tool must be stated only when the requirement makes it essential. A current location inside an allowed area satisfies onsite, hybrid, and attendance terms. Failing criterion IDs are counted in reports. A skill keyword alone does not establish years, proficiency, or ownership in a required role context. Dates use the plan's explicit `as_of` date, and overlapping jobs must not double-count tenure.

The location rubric distinguishes allowed locations, remote scope, office expectations, current-residence requirements, and relocation. Multiple locations are OR alternatives; a specific city or metropolitan area also covers any location within about 100 miles (160 km) of it. Office location is not proof of willingness to commute; residence is not proof of citizenship or authorization. Offered relocation can permit an outside candidate with explicit willingness to relocate for an onsite/hybrid role. For remote roles tied to named geography, current residence in an allowed area is required and relocation willingness does not waive that filter. Remote roles with no stated geography do not acquire an invented country filter. Text controls contradictions with metadata, and explicit attendance terms remain in effect. The gate receives the allowed locations as an explicit OR list, each marked as a city or metro (about 100 miles around it) or a country, state, or region (anywhere inside it), with work-arrangement notes such as "(remote)" separated so they cannot narrow the geography. A candidate matching any alternative passes. A broader stated location lying entirely within an alternative passes; one that contains an allowed area but is much larger (a country for a city role) makes the gate partial.

Stage two runs only when stage one returns `pass` or `partial`. It is a separate call to the same model at the same effort and does not receive the first-stage verdict, search rank, or provider score. Because it uses the same model and is conditioned on stage one, the two stages form a two-stage judge, not independent reviewers. Role context includes the title, work summary, effective must-haves (without source quotes), relevant experience-duration statements with their source quotes and effective interpretations, and the original job description for scope and level. It excludes disqualifiers, location, preferences, and the gate's verdicts. The reviewer is told not to re-gate on original job-description wording that the interpretation removed or relaxed, and not to penalize missing evidence outside the four profile fields. Original ranges supply level context, never a hard upper bound. It assesses exactly one factor: experience appropriate to performing the role at its scope and level, returning `pass` (adequate, appropriately scoped experience and competence, stated or reasonably implied; 1.0), `partial` (relevant experience at roughly the role's level whose adequacy is only partly shown; 0.5), or `fail` (underqualified or obviously overqualified; 0), with a one-sentence description. `pass` means sufficient, not exceptional.

Underqualification is a final experience-fit failure, labeled separately with `underqualified=true` and a `fail` verdict. In the Lite v1 staging runs under the previous prompt, every experience failure was an overqualification call (106 of 106 in deep, 108 of 108 in turbo) and none of the 9,561 candidates that passed the gate in deep failed for lack of experience, so stage two effectively chose only between 1.0 and 0.5. The prompt now states that passing the gate does not establish adequacy and names the failure: the candidate's current or most recent sustained role is at least two levels below the role, the role requires leading people or a function and the profile shows no such leadership, the role's core work appears only in internships, coursework, side projects, or a brief stint, or the candidate's relevant experience is far narrower in scope than the role. One level below, or adjacent experience that makes adequacy genuinely plausible, is `partial`. Missing preferences, evidence outside the four profile fields, and requirements the interpretation removed never make a candidate underqualified. A candidate cannot be both underqualified and obviously overqualified.

Obvious overqualification is a final experience-fit failure, labeled separately with `obviously_overqualified=true` and a `fail` verdict. It is judged leniently and defers toward pass: it applies only when the candidate's current or most recent sustained role is at least two levels above the role (for example a C-level executive, VP, partner, managing director, or founder-CEO of a company of real scale for an individual-contributor, associate, or junior role). One level above the role, hands-on career experience at the role's level, a role at or near the candidate's level, or a senior title on a small team doing the role's work are not overqualification. Years, degrees, and titles alone never establish it, and debatable cases are judged on experience adequacy instead. Age, salary expectations, motivation, boredom, willingness to accept a role, and protected traits must not be inferred.

Function/domain/level contextualize that single factor rather than creating independent axes. Geography, optional preferences, pedigree, personality, and generic attractiveness are not additional factors. Stage-two uncertainty never rescues a failed or unknown hard gate. Both stages use `gpt-6-luna`/medium, strict JSON schemas, and their own system prompt that treats source/profile text as untrusted data. The prompts' worked examples come only from occupations that appear in none of the 290 corpus roles (copy editing, court reporting, land surveying, bookkeeping, claims adjusting, veterinary and litigation support), so the judge is not calibrated on the roles it scores. Outputs are deliberately short to limit output tokens; judges do not return evidence quotes, so verdicts are auditable through descriptions but not mechanically grounded. Requests send a `prompt_cache_key` per role and stage, place fixed instructions and role context before the candidate profile, and run the first call per key alone before fanning out so later calls hit a warm cache. No external browsing or enrichment is performed during judging.

## Precision and top-1 relevance

For a scored candidate at original rank i:

- `y_i = 0` if any must-have is contradicted or has no related support, an explicit disqualifier applies, or location fails. The experience reviewer does not run.
- A gate `partial` (nothing contradicted, but some must-have only plausibly implied by closely adjacent experience) runs the experience reviewer and caps `y_i` at 0.5.
- After a passed gate, the experience reviewer judges only experience fit: `pass` maps to `y_i = 1.0`, `partial` to `y_i = 0.5`, and `fail` (underqualification or obvious overqualification) to `y_i = 0`.
- An adequately experienced candidate passes even without optional preferences. Missing hard-constraint evidence remains a first-stage failure, distinct from partial experience fit after a passed gate.
- Duplicates and unfilled slots in a successful short response earn zero. Search/judge errors are incomplete observations, never synthetic negative labels.

`P@K = sum(y_i for i=1..K) / K` for K in 10, 50, 100, 200. Because credit can be fractional, this is **graded precision** under the requested metric names. The denominator never shrinks to the number returned or the number with complete profiles. Top-1 relevance `= y_1` (zero for an empty successful search) is precision at rank 1 and can be 0, 0.5, or 1.0. It is not recall; the metric was renamed from `r@1` so it is not read as one. For example, a first-ranked `partial` followed by a `pass` gives top-1 relevance 0.5 and P@10=0.15 when all remaining slots score zero.

Zero-padding to 200 is a deliberate choice: the benchmark asks for 200 candidates, and a provider that returns fewer earns nothing for the missing slots. Exa's public people search, for example, returns at most 100 per request, which caps its P@200 at 0.5. Reports therefore show `returned_count` per search and `avg_candidates_returned` per aggregate next to P@K, so a low P@200 can be read as few results or poor ones.

The fixed policy is versioned as `experience-fit-v1` and included, with the numeric credit map, in every plan and report. Plans also freeze the dataset interpretation policy. Unknown hard constraints fail rather than withhold metrics. Only operationally incomplete/invalid judgments withhold metrics. Earlier plans are rejected by the engine/plan hash checks; generate a new plan after this evaluator change.

All requested repetitions and roles must be scoreable before headline quality means are published. Per-role and per-category results remain visible in incomplete reports so failures can be diagnosed. With equal planned repetition counts, overall quality is the arithmetic mean across role-searches, equivalent to the macro mean over roles. Each category has five roles in the full corpus; subset reports are not automatically category balanced beyond the round-robin selection order.

## Latency

Primary latency uses a monotonic client timer around each search HTTP request through receipt of its response. It includes network/transport time and excludes local response normalization, pacing, job extraction, candidate judging, and artifact I/O. API-reported and backend-reported durations are retained separately; overlapping spans are not added together.

Per-role latency is the fastest successful pull: the shortest measured duration among the role's successful search repetitions. Failed attempts and retries never count toward it. With one repetition, that is one observation. Global/category averages are the mean of per-role fastest pulls over successful requests only and always show the successful/expected counts. Failed and unattempted searches have null latency in public summaries; attempt elapsed times remain in private execution records. Resume cache hits reuse original measurements and do not create zero-duration observations.

The harness controls request pacing but cannot guarantee an idle service or a cold server cache. Search modes are separate explicit plans. A recommended comparison uses the same selected roles, three or more repetitions, the same account/source permissions, and documented execution windows. Never interpret source compatibility or synthetic tests as live production validation.

## Reproducibility and failure behavior

Plans hash selected snapshots, effective rubrics, exact request bodies, model/reasoning policy, prompts/schemas/code, scoring settings, and the evaluation date. Changed inputs require a new plan/run. Provider model identifiers and token usage are retained for each completed judgment. A model alias can evolve and model outputs are nondeterministic; hashes and stored judgments make the observed run auditable, not mathematically repeatable across future model versions.

Searches run sequentially with default admission spacing of 300/45 seconds; judges use bounded concurrency. State is atomically marked started before a paid call. Completed records have output hashes and are reused. Failed or uncertain attempts require explicit recovery flags, and a private attempt history is retained. Requests use per-attempt idempotency keys; Wrangle's billing idempotency behavior is not assumed to provide a replayable search-response cache. Do not automatically retry an uncertain request with a new key.

Source or judge errors stop further search work, cancel queued judgments where possible, and produce an incomplete report. A judgment that fails local validation receives one in-unit repair request with its draft; a second rejection is a definite failure, retried only with `--retry-failed judge`. Retry flags are per stage, so a judge retry never re-sends a search. A search whose returned profiles all lack experience data is rejected before judging as a probable response-field mismatch. Schema failures, verdicts inconsistent with their failing criteria, model fallback, silent mode/limit changes, or invalid identities are errors. They are not low-relevance candidates. Manual rubric corrections belong in a new version after a corpus is frozen, with original values and reasons retained.

## Interpretation

The current 290 rubrics are model-generated reference specifications with mechanical validation, targeted review, and documented user-directed interpretations, not human gold labels or employer-confirmed corrections. All 32 recorded source questions are resolved; source quotations and prior interpretations remain available. Hard-gate failures can still be dominated by thin profiles. Publish candidates returned, per-field profile coverage per provider, gate-failure rates, underqualification and overqualification counts, and the pass/partial/fail distribution alongside graded precision. Before a public comparison, a category-balanced human calibration set would improve validity, particularly for responsibility/scope and general-area judgments.
